Grond is the name of two weapons in J. R. R. Tolkien’s Middle-earth: Morgoth’s great mace in The Silmarillion, and the battering ram used against Minas Tirith in The Lord of the Rings. The ram was named in remembrance of the earlier weapon.[1][3]

Morgoth’s mace

The First Age

The first Grond was Morgoth’s great mace, known as the Hammer of the Underworld. In The Silmarillion, he carries it into his duel with Fingolfin outside Angband. Its blows tear pits into the ground, sending up smoke and fire. Fingolfin keeps slipping clear of the descending weapon, until exhaustion overtakes him.[1]

Even then, the duel is no easy victory for Morgoth. Fingolfin wounds him seven times and, before dying, strikes his foot. The Dark Lord carries wounds that never fully heal. Grond belongs to a story of terrible power, but also of the courage to stand before it.[2]

The battering ram

The Third Age

The Grond brought against Minas Tirith is a different weapon: a vast battering ram made in Mordor. It bears the old name in deliberate remembrance of Morgoth’s mace. The connection is a legacy of destruction, rather than the return of the same object.[3]

Tolkien describes a ram roughly a hundred feet long, with a wolf’s head of black steel and enchantments intended for ruin. It hangs from enormous chains, hauled into position by beasts and worked by mountain-trolls. Everything about it serves one purpose: breaking the Great Gate.[3]

Siege of Minas Tirith

15 March, Third Age 3019

During the siege of Minas Tirith, Grond reaches the entrance to the city. The first impact fails to break it. Then, under the Witch-king’s command, the ram strikes three more times. On the last blow, lightning flashes and the gate gives way.[4]

In the book, Gandalf waits at the broken entrance to confront the Witch-king. The arrival of the Rohirrim turns the battle outward again. Grond has accomplished its task, but the fall of a gate does not settle the fate of a city.[5]

Aftermath

After the War of the Ring

The broken gate also has an afterlife. Aragorn receives Gondor’s crown before its ruins. Later, Gimli and Dwarves from Erebor replace it with gates of mithril and steel. The story carries on from the blow to the rebuilding: even Grond’s work does not last forever.[5]

Film and fandom

The 2003 film adaptation of The Return of the King gives Grond a whole wolf-shaped body. In the extended edition, Gothmog summons it after a smaller ram fails. The scene turns a siege engine’s arrival into an entrance worthy of a monster.[6]

Years later, fans gave the ram another life. Know Your Meme documents Grond posts in the r/lotrmemes community as early as 2018. A weapon built for one overwhelming act became the subject of an equally committed running joke.[7]
